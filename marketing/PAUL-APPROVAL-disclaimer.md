# For Paul — one paragraph to approve

**What this is:** before the firm can measure where its website visitors come
from, the disclaimer page has to say so. This is the wording proposed. Nothing is
switched on until you approve it.

**Where it goes:** `donovan.law/disclaimer`, as a new section immediately before
COPYRIGHT. It follows the same shape as the Cloudflare Turnstile paragraph
already on that page.

**Why the second paragraph matters:** the Turnstile section promises that
Cloudflare does *not* use its data for advertising. Google and Meta do. Saying so
explicitly is the difference between a disclosure and an implied promise the firm
cannot keep.

> Google's tools operate in a restricted mode that does not identify you. The

### What visitors will see

A small bar at the bottom of the page, once, on their first visit:

```
We use cookies to understand how visitors use this site and to
measure our advertising. Declining does not affect anything on
the site.   Privacy Policy

                          [ Decline ]      [ Accept ]
```

They answer once. Whichever they choose is remembered — nobody is asked twice.
Declining changes nothing about how the site works.

### Three things worth knowing

- **Nothing is tracked before someone accepts.** Not a soft version of that — the Meta pixel is not loaded at all, and Google runs in a restricted mode that sets no cookies and cannot identify anyone.
- **Decline is as easy as Accept.** Same size, same prominence, and Decline comes first. That is a legal requirement in Europe and a reasonable posture everywhere.
- **They can change their mind.** The *change your cookie choice* link in the paragraph above reopens the question.
