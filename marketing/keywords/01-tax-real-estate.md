# Keywords — tax & real estate (the practice as it exists)

Input for Google Keyword Planner. **Volume and CPC columns are deliberately
empty** — nobody has pulled real figures yet, and a made-up number in a budget
conversation is worse than a blank one. Planner is free and needs no campaign:
Ads → Tools → Keyword Planner → *Get search volume and forecasts*.

Every campaign here is a separate budget. That is the whole reason to split them:
the money dial lives at campaign level.

**Match types:** default to **phrase** `"…"`. Broad match with no conversion
history will spend a budget on adjacent nonsense before it learns anything. Exact
`[…]` for the few terms where intent is unambiguous and expensive.

## How Google matches — read before adding "fallback" keywords

Matching is far smarter than it looks, and the risk runs the **opposite** way to
the one people expect: Google matches too BROADLY, not too narrowly.

**Handled automatically. Do not add these — they only clutter the account:**

| | Example |
|---|---|
| Typos and misspellings | "attourney", "segragation" |
| Hyphens and punctuation | `cost-segregation` = `cost segregation` |
| Plurals and stemming | attorney / attorneys |
| Word order | "1031 exchange attorney" / "attorney 1031 exchange" |
| Function words | "attorney for irs audit" |
| Same-meaning synonyms | attorney / lawyer |

**Not handled — these need their own keyword:**

- **Jargon shorthand.** "cost seg", "OIC", "1031" alone, "REPS". Different words, not variants. Added above.
- **Genuinely different concepts.** "IRS audit" vs "IRS examination".
- **Spanish.** Google does not cross-match languages. Spanish traffic needs its own keywords, its own ads, and probably its own campaign — worth doing, since Paula is bilingual.

**The Search Terms report replaces all guessing within two weeks.** It shows the
real queries that triggered the ads. Promote the good ones to keywords, add the
junk as negatives. This list is a starting position that evidence corrects — not
a prediction that has to be right.

## Negative keywords

Not optional. Without them a third of any legal budget goes to people who were
never going to hire anybody. **Negatives matter far more than fallback keywords.**

Note the two levels. Some negatives that are right for one campaign are actively
wrong for another.

### Account level — applies everywhere

**Wrong intent** — not hiring anyone:
```
jobs          salary        career        hiring        resume        intern
free          cheap         pro bono      no cost       diy           "do it yourself"
template      sample        form          pdf           letter sample
course        school        degree        certification exam          cpe
what is       how to        definition    meaning       explained
```

**Research, not retention** — reading about lawyers rather than hiring one:
```
reddit        quora         wikipedia     forum         avvo          justia
findlaw       lawyers.com   best          top 10        reviews of    vs
```

**Wrong service entirely** — the software and the agency, not counsel:
```
turbotax      h&r block     jackson hewitt
irs phone number            irs office    irs address   where's my refund
```

**⚠️ Practice areas the firm does not offer.** This one is specific to this
account and it is the easy money to save. Because Google matches same-meaning
terms, a tax campaign will pull in adjacent legal work. Every one of these is a
click paid for work that cannot be taken:
```
bankruptcy    personal injury   divorce lawyer   custody   dui   criminal defense
immigration   green card        visa             deportation
harassment    wrongful termination               discrimination
```

**Immigration and harassment stay on this list until Paul answers the competence
question** (`../OPEN-QUESTIONS.md` #1). If either becomes a real practice area
they come off — and until then, buying those clicks is worse than wasteful.

### Campaign level — NOT account level

**State names must never be account-level negatives.** Campaign 2 deliberately
targets "new york residency audit" and "massachusetts residency audit". A blanket
negative on state names would switch off the sharpest campaign in the account.

If geography needs narrowing, do it with **location targeting**, not negatives.

**`near me`** is a judgement call, not a default: keep it if targeting is local,
drop it if nationwide. It is not bad, it is *local*.

**Competitor names** — some firms bid on rivals. It works, and Florida Bar
advertising rules have views. **Paul's call.** Not on any list until he says.

## Before any of this becomes a campaign

1. **Pull real volume and CPC.** Every number above is blank on purpose.
2. **Check the landing pages actually convert.** The calculators are the strongest asset in the account, but a calculator with no obvious next step is a dead end. Each needs a visible route to booking.
3. **`pre immigration tax planning` has no page.** Either write one or drop the term — pointing an ad at a page that does not exist is how you buy a bounce.
4. **Geography.** Nationwide multiplies both volume and cost. Undecided; Paul's.
5. **Ad copy → FL Bar review.** Past-results claims need disclaimers, and "specialist" is restricted. Paul is a CPA and that is a factual, defensible differentiator — lead with it.
