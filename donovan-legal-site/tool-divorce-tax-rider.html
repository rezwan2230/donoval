Educational draft — not legal advice, not for signature. This tool is provided by Donovan Legal PLLC for educational and illustrative purposes only. It assembles model tax provisions from the choices you make, summarizes federal and state law at a high level, may not reflect current law or your circumstances, and produces a draft that has not been reviewed by any lawyer and must not be signed or attached to any agreement without review by tax counsel retained by the party. Using it does not create an attorney–client relationship. Every bracket is a decision counsel must make.As of: law summarized as of September 2026 · model reviewed September 5, 2026 · the draft prints and copies with an educational watermark and header.

A marital settlement agreement is the family lawyer’s document, and it is very good at what it does. What it does not do is tax: who files how for the year of the divorce and who gets the savings; who bears a deficiency on the joint years and who controls the audit; who claims the children and how the Form 8332 actually gets signed; the one sentence that keeps the § 121 exclusion for the spouse who moves out; the QDRO’s drafting, timing and survivor benefit; the § 408(d)(6) transfer sequenced after signing; the basis records; the carryforwards that cannot be assigned; the S corporation’s year of transfer; and the state layer — residency after the divorce, state conformity of filing status, which state taxes the retirement distributions and the later sale, state carryovers, real-property withholding, the state credits; the foreign layer where a spouse, an account, a pension or a trust is abroad; the gift, estate and transfer taxes (§ 2516, beneficiary designations, the marital deduction that ends at the decree); and the property, documentary, sales-tax and business-tax items that attach to what is being moved. This tool assembles those provisions as a rider — a schedule the family lawyer attaches to the agreement they drafted, in your state.

How the rider is used

The family lawyer drafts the settlement agreement; this rider is attached to it as a schedule and controls on tax matters. Nothing in it changes the parenting plan, the support award or the division the family lawyer negotiated — it says how each of those will be reported, who bears which tax, and what has to happen, in what order, for the tax result the parties bargained for to survive. The draft above is an educational starting point built from your answers. The firm’s engagement is to review the facts, make the decisions the brackets leave open, coordinate with your family lawyer, and deliver a signed rider — retained by you, so your lawyer’s tax disclaimer stays intact.

The seven divorce tools on this site feed the rider: the balance sheet sets the equalization, the home tool sets the residence payment, the retirement tool sets the QDRO terms, the carryforward tool sets the attribute schedule.

The settlement agreement your lawyer drafts is missing a chapter. This is it.

Family lawyers disclaim tax, correctly, and their agreements show it: the joint exclusion lost to a closing date, the IRA cashed out to pay the other side, the carryforward “assigned” to the wrong spouse, the dependency with no Form 8332 mechanics. This rider is the chapter the agreement needs. The draft above is educational; the firm’s engagement turns it into a reviewed, signed rider delivered to your lawyer to attach — retained by you, so your lawyer’s file stays clean and the tax provisions get a tax lawyer.
