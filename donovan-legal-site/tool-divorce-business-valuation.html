Ninety-nine percent of divorces settle, most at mediation, and the business is where they get stuck. The two fights are always the same: discounts (marketability, minority, key person) and the standard of value — fair market value, fair value, or the value of the business to the spouse who keeps it — which each state resolves differently, and which judges bend toward what seems fair whether the statute says so or not. This tool values the business on fundamentals, applies your state’s posture to each contested choice, shows what each is worth in dollars, and assembles a draft Calculation of Value report in the AICPA SSVS calculation-engagement form. Two levels: the calculation you run here is educational and yours to bring to the table; the firm’s fixed-fee engagement verifies the inputs, sources the market data, makes the judgment calls the draft flags, and issues the report under a CPA’s signature — the pre-litigation alternative to a forensic report. That is the map you need at mediation. If the case still needs an expert afterwards, you will know exactly what to ask them to prove.

Enterprise value is built three ways — capitalized normalized earnings, a market-multiple cross-check, adjusted net assets as a floor — then adjusted for goodwill, tax-affecting and discounts under Florida, Massachusetts, New York, California or Texas rules.

How your state decides the fights

Why the forensic report can wait

A forensic accountant is retained to value and, when it comes to it, to testify. Both are worth paying for when the case is going to trial. Most cases are not. What the parties need at mediation is a defensible calculated value and a clear view of which assumptions are really in dispute — because in a divorce the standard of value is not the textbook’s, it is the state’s, and it bends toward equity. Massachusetts rejects discounts where the spouse keeps the business; California divides personal goodwill; Florida and Texas exclude it; New York makes the difficulty of valuing the asset a statutory factor. Knowing that before the retainer is paid is the difference between a report that settles the case and one that starts the fight.
