## 1. INTAKE PROCESSES BY PRACTICE AREA

---

### 1.1 Personal Injury / MVA
*Covers: Motor vehicle accidents, slip & fall, medical malpractice, wrongful death, product liability*

#### Standard Intake Questionnaire (Actual Questions Asked)

**Client Identification**
- Full legal name, date of birth, last four digits of SSN
- Home address, home phone, cell phone, email
- Emergency contact and relationship
- How were you referred to this firm?

**Employment & Financial**
- Current employer, supervisor name and contact
- Compensation (hourly/salary), typical weekly schedule
- Estimated wage loss due to injury
- Are you on Medicare or Medicaid?
- Health insurance carrier and policy number
- Are you enrolled in state disability benefits? If so, how long?
- Gross weekly pay

**Insurance Information**
- Your auto insurance carrier, policy number, agent name/address
- Underinsured/uninsured motorist (UIM) coverage limits
- Do you have MedPay coverage? If yes, how much?
- At-fault party's insurance company, adjuster name, claim number
- Defendant's vehicle/property owner information

**Incident Facts**
- Date of accident (exact)
- Location: city, county, state, street/cross-street
- Type of accident (circle): car accident / slip & fall / dog bite / product liability / medical malpractice
- Describe accident in detail (prompt for diagram where possible)
- All vehicle occupants (list driver first)
- All witnesses: names, addresses, contact info
- Were you cited at the scene? If yes, for what?
- Did you give a recorded statement? To whom? What was said?
- Any pictures taken of scene/property/injuries? Who has them?
- Was your vehicle damaged? Any repair estimates taken? By whom?

**Bodily Injuries**
- Client's initial injuries (be very specific)
- How have your injuries changed your lifestyle? (this is vital — prompt for specific impact)
- All treating providers: name, address, phone, dates of treatment, future appointments, bill amounts
- Were diagnostic tests performed? Type, date, results?
- Current symptoms and pain levels

**Past Medical & Litigation History**
- Have you been in a previous accident? List details.
- Past serious illnesses or injuries?
- Hospitalization in the last 10 years?
- Have you ever filed a claim or lawsuit for personal injuries or workers' comp? (subject matter, parties, court number, settlement amount)
- Prior claims involving same body part areas (critical to disclose)

**Source:** [Lucas Law Personal Injury Questionnaire (PDF)](https://www.lucaslaw.com/images/client-forms/LucasLaw-Personal-Injury-Client-Questionnaire.pdf), [Petrillo & Goldberg MVA Intake](https://www.petrilloandgoldberg.com/forms/mva-intake/), [CasePeer PI Intake Guide](https://www.casepeer.com/guides/personal-injury-client-intake-form/)

---

#### Qualification Criteria (What Makes a Good Case)

| Factor | Good Case Indicators |
|--------|---------------------|
| **Liability** | Clear fault by defendant; police report supports client's version; witnesses corroborate |
| **Damages** | Significant injury requiring ongoing treatment; documented wage loss; high impact on daily life |
| **Insurance coverage** | Defendant has adequate policy limits; client has UIM coverage if defendant under-insured |
| **Credibility** | Client is consistent, likable; no major criminal history; stable life circumstances |
| **Causation** | Direct link between incident and injuries; gap in treatment is small or explainable |
| **Case value** | Medical bills + lost wages + pain/suffering justify time and litigation costs |
| **Urgency** | Within statute of limitations with sufficient time to investigate |

The single most important evaluation: *case value vs. cost to prosecute*. Catastrophic injuries (paralysis, TBI, amputation, wrongful death) are highly desirable. Cases with policy limits of only $15,000 require careful cost/benefit analysis.

**Source:** [Plaintiff Magazine — Initial Intake Checklist](https://plaintiffmagazine.com/recent-issues/item/initial-intake-a-checklist-of-factors-to-consider)

---

#### Disqualification Criteria (Turn-Away Triggers)

- Statute of limitations has expired (generally 2–3 years depending on state; 6 months for government entities)
- Pre-existing injury to same body part without adequate documentation separating it
- Client gave recorded statement admitting fault before retaining attorney
- Client unreliable, inconsistent, or with extensive prior personal injury claims (serial claimant)
- Defendant has no insurance and no assets
- Minor impact with soft-tissue-only injuries — marginal value
- Client is uncooperative with treatment or refuses to follow medical advice
- Prior undisclosed claims in the same body area with large settlements already received
- Criminal history that would destroy credibility in front of jury

---

#### Conflict of Interest Checks Required
- Run names of client, defendant(s), and all insurance companies through firm conflict database
- Check for any prior representation of at-fault party or their insurer
- Check for any representation of adverse parties in concurrent matters

---

#### Documents Collected at Intake
- Police/accident report
- Photos of scene, vehicle damage, injuries
- Insurance ID cards (both parties)
- Medical records release authorization
- Declaration page of client's auto insurance policy
- Any prior claims/settlement documentation
- Employment records / pay stubs (for lost wages)
- Recorded statement transcripts (if already given)

---

#### Fee Structure
- **Standard contingency fee:** 33.3% pre-lawsuit settlement; 40% if lawsuit filed; up to 45% if case goes to appeal
- No upfront fee — attorney advances costs and recoups from settlement
- Hard costs (filing fees, experts, medical records) deducted from settlement separately
- Client signs a contingency fee agreement and medical records release at engagement

**Source:** [Mighty — Standard Personal Injury Fees](https://www.mighty.com/blog/standard-personal-injury-fees), [GJEL Accident Attorneys](https://www.gjel.com/contingency-fee)

---

#### Intake Duration & Format
- **Phone screening:** 5–15 minutes (pre-screen to determine if case viable before full intake)
- **Full intake appointment:** 45–90 minutes (in-person or video)
- Many firms use a two-stage process: brief phone call to assess basic facts, followed by in-person or detailed digital intake form completion

---

#### Urgency / Statute of Limitations
- **General negligence (most states):** 2–3 years from date of injury
- **Government entity claims:** 45–180 days notice (varies by state and municipality) — this is the most common killer of otherwise viable cases
- **Wrongful death:** Often starts from date of death, not date of incident
- **Minors:** Clock typically starts at 18th birthday
- **Discovery rule:** For injuries not immediately apparent, clock may start at discovery
- **Medical malpractice:** Often 2–3 years from discovery; some states have additional caps

**The urgency flag is one of the most critical intake screening elements.** Any case within 60–90 days of SOL should be escalated to an attorney immediately.

---

#### Referral Patterns
- Cases with no insurance or minimal policy limits → refer to workers' comp attorneys or ERISA counsel if work-related
- Medical malpractice (specialized) → dedicated med-mal firms if general PI firm doesn't handle
- Product liability (mass torts) → mass tort firms or co-counsel arrangements
- Clients who clearly want to file for bankruptcy → bankruptcy attorney referral
- Cases outside SOL → decline with written non-engagement letter

### 1.2 Family Law
*Covers: Divorce, custody, child support, adoption, prenuptial agreements, domestic violence*

#### Standard Intake Questionnaire

**Personal Information**
- Full legal name, spouse's full name (including maiden name)
- Current address, phone numbers, email
- Date of birth for client and spouse
- Social security numbers (last 4 digits minimum for conflict check)

**Marriage Details**
- Date of marriage, place of marriage, marriage certificate available?
- Have you or spouse been previously married? Dates and how ended (death/divorce)?
- Court and county of prior divorces

**Separation Details**
- Are you currently separated? Date of separation?
- Any formal separation agreements in place?
- Do you and spouse currently live together?
- Is your spouse aware you are considering divorce? Are they willing to agree?
- Does your spouse currently have an attorney? If so, who?

**Children Information**
- Names and dates of birth of all children
- Which children were born of this marriage vs. prior relationships
- Is anyone currently pregnant?
- Children's current living situation
- Any existing custody arrangements or court orders?
- Special needs or problems with any children?
- Will there be agreement on custody? If yes, primary custody with whom?
- What are your goals for custody and parenting time?

**Financial Information**
- Employment status and income (both parties)
- Can you furnish an income/expense statement?
- Gross monthly income, net monthly income
- Joint bank accounts, investment accounts
- Debts and liabilities shared with spouse

**Property and Assets (complete inventory)**
- Marital residence: address, title holder(s), who made down payment, current payments, approximate value, mortgage balance
- Other real estate: same details
- Automobiles: year/model, title holder, who has possession, liens, value vs. balance
- Retirement accounts (401k, pension, IRA)
- Business interests — any businesses owned individually or jointly?
- Life insurance policies

**Legal Agreements**
- Prenuptial or postnuptial agreements?
- Existing wills/estate plans affected by divorce?
- Any prior divorce filings against this spouse (when, where, how terminated)?

**Safety / Urgent Issues**
- Any instances of domestic violence or abuse?
- Any immediate safety concerns or restraining orders needed?
- Are there any firearms in the household?
- Immigration status of either party (affects available remedies)

**Goals**
- Primary goals for the divorce process
- Any other information relevant to the case?

**Source:** [Content Snare Divorce Intake Template](https://contentsnare.com/form-templates/divorce-intake-questionnaire/), [Evans Family Law Questionnaire (PDF)](https://www.evansfamilylawgroup.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/09/Family-Law-Questionnaire_F4.pdf), [Peraica Divorce Intake (PDF)](https://peraica.com/wp-content/uploads/2017/09/Divorce-Intake-Questionnaire.pdf)

---

#### Qualification Criteria
- Client resides in jurisdiction and meets residency requirements for filing
- Assets exist to divide (or there's clear child custody dispute worth pursuing)
- Client can afford retainer and ongoing fees — OR qualifies for sliding scale/legal aid
- Matter falls within firm's specialty (contested vs. uncontested; high-net-worth vs. standard)
- Domestic violence present → immediate escalation and emergency filing capability confirmed

---

#### Disqualification Criteria
- No assets, no children, and uncontested → refer to legal document prep service or self-help
- Case involves jurisdiction where firm is not licensed
- Conflict of interest (firm represented spouse previously)
- Client cannot afford retainer and doesn't qualify for aid
- Complex international assets or multinational custody → may require specialist referral

---

#### Conflict of Interest Checks
- Run both spouses through conflict database (firm cannot represent both; check if spouse is a former client or adverse party in another matter)
- Check children's names if firm has prior juvenile matters involving the family

---

#### Documents Collected at Intake
- Marriage certificate
- Birth certificates for all children
- Proof of income: last 2 years tax returns, 3–6 months of pay stubs
- Bank statements (joint and separate) — 3–6 months
- Retirement/investment account statements
- Property deeds, mortgage statements
- Vehicle titles
- Prenuptial/postnuptial agreement (if any)
- Any existing court orders (custody, support, restraining orders)
- Insurance policies

---

#### Fee Structure
- **Retainer:** $2,500–$5,000 for most contested divorces and custody disputes (national average per LegalMatch)
- **Hourly rate:** $254–$500/hour nationally; higher in major metro areas
- **Evergreen retainer:** Many firms require replenishment before balance hits $0
- **Flat fee:** Some firms offer flat fees for uncontested divorces — typically $1,500–$3,500
- **Contingency NOT allowed** in family law in most states; contingency fees for divorce or custody are generally prohibited

**Source:** [Hopwood Law Offices — Retainer Fees for Family Law](https://hopwoodlawoffices.com/how-to-understand-retainer-fees-for-family-law-attorneys/), [LawPay — Lawyer Retainers](https://www.lawpay.com/about/blog/lawyer-retainers/)

---

#### Intake Duration
- **Phone screening:** 10–20 minutes (assess urgency, safety concerns, basic facts)
- **Initial consultation:** 60–90 minutes (in-person or video)
- **Best practice:** Send questionnaire 48 hours in advance so client can gather documents

---

#### Urgency / Statute of Limitations Considerations
- **Domestic violence / emergency protective orders:** Immediate — same day or next day
- **Child custody disputes with imminent removal threat:** Expedited filing required
- **Divorce filings:** No strict SOL, but separation date matters for property cut-off and support calculations
- **Child support modifications:** Generally can be filed anytime circumstances change materially
- **Adoption:** Complex timelines depending on type (domestic infant, foster care, international)

---

#### Referral Patterns
- High-net-worth with complex business valuations → forensic accountant and business valuation expert
- International custody disputes → Hague Convention specialists
- Bankruptcy alongside divorce → coordinate with bankruptcy attorney
- Immigration complications (visa status affected by divorce) → immigration attorney
- Domestic violence → local DV shelter, victim advocacy organizations
- Clients who can't afford attorney → legal aid, law school clinics, self-help court programs

---

### 1.3 Criminal Defense
*Covers: DUI/DWI, drug charges, assault, theft, white collar, federal crimes*

#### Standard Intake Questionnaire

**Personal History**
- Full legal name, date of birth, race/nationality
- Social security number
- Current address, length of time at address, previous addresses (last 10 years)
- Emergency contact (nearest relative)
- Children: names, location, financially responsible?

**Employment & Financial**
- Current employer, address, supervisor, position, how long employed
- Salary/hourly wage
- Previous employers (last 10 years) — for character witnesses and stability assessment

**Health**
- Overall health status (excellent/good/poor)
- Currently under any physician's care? Describe.
- Addiction issues (alcohol / drugs)? Ever treated? Facility, dates, program completed?
- Allergies

**Education**
- Highest grade completed, vocational training, college, additional skills
- Hobbies (relevant to character witnesses)

**Immigration Status**
- Are you a U.S. citizen? If not, what is your immigration status? (This is critical — criminal convictions can trigger deportation)
- Current visa status; green card holder; DACA recipient?
- Are you currently on probation or pre-trial release on another matter?

**Legal Information**
- Other names / aliases used
- Prior record: original charge, conviction charge, date, location, sentence
- If currently on probation: judge, probation officer, time remaining
- Have you ever been questioned but not arrested in connection with a crime?

**Present Offense**
- Charge(s) filed
- Bond amount / conditions
- Co-defendants
- Date, time, and location of alleged offense
- Description of incident (from client's perspective — be very specific)
- Arresting agency and arresting officer's name
- Was Miranda warning given?
- Did you make any statements? Written or verbal?
- Were you asked to participate in a lineup?
- Were you fingerprinted or photographed?
- Did you ask for an attorney? Was one provided?
- Were you physically threatened or mentally harassed by police?

**Witnesses**
- List all witnesses to events: name, address, phone
- Character/mitigating witnesses: employer, social workers, friends
- Who will testify for you? Against you?

**For DUI Specifically:**
- BAC reading (breath or blood)
- 10-day deadline to request DMV hearing (California); similar short windows in most states
- Prior DUI convictions (affects charges and sentencing)
- Refused chemical test? (triggers automatic license suspension)
- Were you in an accident? Any injuries?
- Field sobriety tests administered? Describe.

**Source:** [Carter & Rader Criminal Intake (PDF)](https://www.carterandrader.com/wp-content/uploads/2020/06/Client-Questionnaire-Criminal.pdf), [Phillips & King Criminal Intake (PDF)](https://www.phillipsking.com/wp-content/uploads/2018/03/Criminal-Intake-Questionnaire.pdf)

---

#### Qualification Criteria
- Firm handles the charge type (misdemeanor vs. felony vs. federal)
- Client is charged and needs representation (not merely under investigation — though early representation is better)
- Client can pay retainer
- Geographic jurisdiction where attorney is licensed and has courtroom relationships

---

#### Disqualification Criteria
- Federal charges at firms that only handle state matters (federal practice is specialized)
- Charges requiring expertise firm lacks (e.g., complex white collar at general criminal defense firm)
- Conflict of interest (representing co-defendant or victim in another matter)
- Client faces capital murder charges if firm doesn't handle capital cases
- Client cannot pay and doesn't qualify for public defender → refer to public defender or legal aid

---

#### Conflict of Interest Checks
- Run defendant name against firm client list
- Check co-defendants — cannot represent both without waiver in most cases
- Check alleged victim's name against client database

---

#### Documents Collected at Intake
- Arrest/booking paperwork
- Bond paperwork and conditions of release
- Any citations or charging documents provided
- Police report (obtain via public records request)
- Any written statements client gave
- Probation/parole documents if client is on supervision
- Prior conviction records (if client has them)
- Character reference names and contacts

---

#### Fee Structure
- **Misdemeanor DUI (flat fee):** $1,500–$5,000 typically
- **Felony (flat fee or retainer):** $5,000–$25,000 retainer for state felonies
- **Federal cases:** $25,000–$75,000 initial retainer (covers pre-trial work only); separate trial retainer of $50,000–$150,000
- **Hourly:** $100–$400/hour for criminal defense nationally; $300–$700/hour federal
- Most criminal defense is flat fee for defined stages of the case
- Payment plans more common in criminal defense than other practice areas

**Source:** [Spodek Law Group — Federal Retainer Requirements](https://www.nyccriminalattorneys.com/retainer-requirements-federal-cases/), [Super Lawyers — Paying for Criminal Defense](https://www.superlawyers.com/resources/criminal-defense/how-to-pay-for-a-criminal-defense-attorney/)

---

#### Intake Duration
- **Initial phone call:** 10–20 minutes (urgency is often high — assess charges, jail status, imminent hearings)
- **In-person consultation:** 45–75 minutes
- Criminal defense often requires 24/7 availability given arrest timing

---

#### Urgency / Statute of Limitations
- **DUI DMV hearing request:** 10 days from arrest in California (similar short windows in most states) — missing this permanently suspends license regardless of criminal outcome
- **First court appearance (arraignment):** Often within 48–72 hours of arrest
- **Speedy trial rights:** 6th Amendment; roughly 70 days in federal court, varies by state
- Statute of limitations on criminal charges: set by prosecution, not relevant for post-arrest intake (but matters for when to report to police)
- **Immigration hold:** If client has immigration detainer, additional extreme urgency

---

#### Referral Patterns
- Federal charges → federal criminal defense specialist
- White collar / RICO / fraud → financial crimes specialists
- Drug charges with addiction component → consider drug court eligibility
- Juvenile charges → juvenile defense specialist
- Immigration consequences → coordinate with immigration attorney
- DUI with injury (potential civil liability) → flag for PI coordination

### 1.4 Immigration
*Covers: Visa applications, green cards, deportation defense, asylum, work permits, naturalization*

#### Standard Intake Questionnaire

**Basic Identification**
- Full legal name, date of birth, country of birth, country of citizenship
- A-number (Alien Registration Number) if any
- Current address, phone, email

**Immigration History**
- Date first entered the U.S.
- All entries and exits from the U.S. (dates, whether inspected by immigration, visa held on entry, when authorized stay expired)
- Visa type on last entry (or "none" / entered without inspection)
- Current immigration status
- Prior visas, interviews at U.S. consulates or embassies
- Any time spent in immigration detention?
- Interactions with immigration agents / CBP at the border

**Removal/Deportation History**
- Ever deported or received voluntary departure? Describe what happened.
- Ever been in immigration court?
- Ever signed a form before leaving the U.S.?
- Prior removal orders?

**Immigration Benefits Sought/Applied For**
- Have you applied for any immigration benefit before? (permanent residency, asylum, DACA, TPS, VAWA, U visa, T visa, etc.)
- If so, when, what paperwork, what was the result?
- Has any paperwork been filed on your behalf?

**Family Ties**
- Are you married? To whom (name, citizenship/immigration status, date married)?
- Former spouses (name, status, how marriage ended)?
- Parents and grandparents: U.S. citizens?
- Children: names, ages, citizenship status
- Other immediate family in the U.S.?
- Family members in military service? (Parole-in-place eligibility)

**Work Authorization**
- Work authorized? How?
- Current employer
- Work history in the U.S.

**Criminal History**
- Any arrests, indictments, convictions in the U.S. or abroad?
- Sentence received?
- (Criminal history is critical — many offenses trigger deportability or inadmissibility)

**Asylum/Fear-Based Claims**
- Do you have any reason to fear going back to your country? Who do you fear and why?
- Were you a victim of domestic abuse by spouse, parent, or child?
- Has your country's government or groups persecuted you based on race, religion, nationality, political opinion, or membership in a particular social group?

**Source:** [ILRC Sample Intake Form (PDF)](https://www.ilrc.org/sites/default/files/resources/ilrc_sample_intake_form_-_sept_2019_0.pdf), [National Immigrant Justice Center Asylum Intake](https://immigrantjustice.org/sites/default/files/BestPracticesManual_2.%20NIJC%20Asylum%20Project%20Intake%20Questions.pdf)

---

#### Qualification Criteria

| Case Type | What Makes It Viable |
|-----------|---------------------|
| **Family-based petition** | USC or LPR petitioner; petitioner is immediate relative (fastest) or in a visa preference category |
| **Employment-based** | Employer willing to sponsor; job qualifies under preference category; employer can pay prevailing wage |
| **Asylum** | Filed within 1 year of entry (or exceptional/changed circumstances apply); credible fear of persecution on enumerated grounds |
| **Cancellation of removal** | LPR for 5+ years AND 7 years in U.S.; OR 10 years continuous presence (non-LPR) with qualifying family member |
| **DACA** | Brought to U.S. before age 16; continuous residence since June 15, 2007 |
| **U Visa** | Victim of qualifying crime; suffered abuse; cooperated with law enforcement |
| **Naturalization** | 5 years as LPR (3 if married to USC); continuous residence; good moral character; passed civics/language test |

---

#### Disqualification Criteria
- Asylum: filed more than 1 year after entry with no exceptional circumstances (hard bar)
- Prior order of removal with no motion to reopen/reconsider grounds
- Aggravated felony conviction (typically renders ineligible for most relief)
- Client has no legal pathway based on family, employment, or humanitarian basis
- Prior approved petition that was denied due to fraud (permanent bar in many cases)
- Matter requires specific specialization firm lacks (complex federal litigation, bond hearings)

---

#### Conflict of Interest Checks
- Check opposing parties in removal proceedings (DHS attorneys involved)
- Check if firm represented opposing party in same proceedings
- Check family members who may be adverse in custody-immigration intersection

---

#### Documents Collected at Intake
- Passport (all pages with stamps)
- Visa documents and I-94 arrival/departure records
- Notice to Appear (NTA) — if in removal proceedings
- Prior immigration applications and approval/denial notices
- Birth certificates (certified translations if not in English)
- Marriage certificate and divorce decrees if applicable
- Police reports / court records for any criminal history
- Any correspondence from USCIS or immigration court
- Employment records and tax returns (for adjustment of status)
- Country condition evidence for asylum

---

#### Fee Structure
- **Flat fee by case type** is most common in immigration:
  - Simple petition (I-130 family): $1,500–$3,000
  - Adjustment of status (green card): $2,000–$5,000
  - Naturalization: $500–$2,500
  - Asylum: $3,000–$8,000 (contested)
  - Deportation defense: $3,000–$10,000+ depending on complexity
  - Employment-based sponsorship: $5,000–$15,000 (employer often pays)
- **Hourly** for complex matters: $150–$400/hour
- Filing fees paid separately to USCIS

---

#### Intake Duration
- **Phone screening:** 10–15 minutes (assess status, type of relief, urgency)
- **Full consultation:** 60–120 minutes (immigration consultations are typically longer due to document complexity)
- Often requires interpreter services for non-English speakers

---

#### Urgency / Key Deadlines
- **Asylum:** 1-year filing deadline from date of entry — extremely strict
- **Responses to USCIS RFEs (Request for Evidence):** 87 days (no extensions in most cases)
- **Motions to reopen:** Must be filed within 90 days of final order
- **Bond hearings:** Within 5 business days of detention in federal custody
- **DAPA/DACA renewals:** 5 months before expiration
- **Notice to Appear (NTA) deadlines:** Dates set by immigration court — missing court = order of removal in absentia

---

#### Referral Patterns
- Criminal defense required alongside immigration → coordinate with criminal defense attorney
- Employment-based with complex labor certification → business immigration specialist
- International adoption with immigration aspects → specialized adoption + immigration attorney
- Victims of trafficking (T visa) → legal aid or specialized NGOs
- Unaccompanied minors → specialized juvenile/immigration attorney
- Cases with complex constitutional issues → ACLU or NILC

### 1.5 Real Estate
*Covers: Residential purchase/sale, commercial transactions, landlord-tenant disputes, zoning*

#### Standard Intake Questionnaire

**Client & Transaction Basics**
- Full name, contact information
- Role in transaction: buyer / seller / landlord / tenant / investor
- Property address and type (single-family, condo, commercial, land)
- Transaction type: purchase / sale / lease / dispute / zoning / title issue
- Estimated purchase price or lease value

**For Residential Purchase/Sale**
- Are you represented by a real estate agent? Agent's name and contact?
- Is there an executed purchase agreement / offer to purchase? (Bring copy)
- Closing date scheduled?
- Is financing involved? Lender and loan type?
- Any contingencies in the agreement? (Inspection, financing, appraisal)
- Title company identified?
- Any known issues with property (structural, title, environmental)?
- Existing liens or encumbrances?
- HOA documents available?

**For Commercial Transactions**
- Type of commercial property (office, retail, industrial, multifamily)
- Intended use of property?
- Existing tenants?
- Environmental due diligence needed?
- Entity structure of buyer/seller?
- 1031 exchange involved?

**For Landlord-Tenant Disputes**
- Are you landlord or tenant?
- Lease in place? (Bring copy)
- Nature of dispute: non-payment / habitability / wrongful eviction / security deposit / lease termination
- Any prior court filings?
- Urgency (eviction timeline)?

**For Zoning/Land Use**
- Current zoning designation
- Proposed use or change
- Municipality/planning board involved

**Source:** [Curcio Law PLLC — First Meeting with NY Real Estate Attorney](https://www.curciolawpllc.com/post/how-to-prepare-for-your-first-meeting-with-a-new-york-real-estate-attorney), [Lulich & Attorneys](https://www.lulich.com/questions-to-ask-your-real-estate-attorney/)

---

#### Qualification Criteria
- Transaction is in state/county where firm practices
- Transaction value justifies attorney fees (very small deals may not be cost-effective)
- Client needs legal services (not just document prep)
- Commercial clients: entity is properly formed; principals have authority to transact

---

#### Disqualification Criteria
- Property is in another state where firm is not licensed
- Both buyer and seller seeking representation from same firm (conflict without consent)
- Client involved in active litigation requiring specialist
- Complex environmental issues beyond firm's expertise

---

#### Conflict of Interest Checks
- Check all parties (buyer, seller, both real estate agents, mortgage lender, title company) against existing client database
- Dual representation (buyer and seller in same transaction) requires explicit written consent and disclosure

---

#### Documents at Intake
**Residential:**
- Purchase agreement / offer to purchase (signed)
- Existing title report or prior title insurance policy
- Most recent property tax bills
- HOA documents and financial statements (if applicable)
- Survey (if available)
- Mortgage commitment letter (for buyer)
- Any disclosure statements from seller
- Inspection report (if completed)

**Commercial:**
- Letter of Intent (LOI)
- Due diligence materials
- Existing leases
- Environmental assessments
- Entity formation documents of buyer/seller

---

#### Fee Structure
- **Flat fee (residential):** $500–$2,500 per transaction depending on region
- **Flat fee (commercial):** $2,500–$10,000+ depending on complexity
- **Hourly (disputes/litigation):** $200–$450/hour
- **Retainer for disputes:** $2,000–$10,000
- Note: In many states (NY, NJ, CT, MA, etc.), attorney representation is customary or required for real estate transactions

---

#### Intake Duration
- **Phone/email screening:** 15–30 minutes
- **Initial consultation:** 30–60 minutes (often can be conducted efficiently with documents in hand)

- Number of company members/owners

- Target company or asset details

- Due diligence timeline?

### 1.7 Estate Planning / Probate
*Covers: Wills, trusts, power of attorney, estate administration, guardianship*

#### Standard Intake Questionnaire

**Client Information**
- Full legal name (Sr./Jr./III if applicable), date of birth
- Address, phone, email
- Social security number
- U.S. citizen? (Non-citizens have additional planning considerations)
- Are you a disabled veteran?
- Current health status (excellent/good/poor); treating physician
- How did you hear about the firm?
- Referred by whom?

**Spouse/Partner (if applicable)**
- Same identification fields as client
- Date of marriage, prenuptial agreement?
- Do you have a prenuptial agreement?

**Prior Documents**
- Do you have a prior will? Trust?
- Are you making payments pursuant to a divorce or property settlement?
- If prior trust exists: full legal name and date of creation

**Family History**
- Names and contact info for parents (living or deceased)
- Siblings (full blood, half-blood; living or deceased)
- Prior marriages: dates, how ended (death/divorce), name of prior spouse

**Children**
- Names, dates of birth, addresses of all children (including stepchildren and from prior marriages)
- Children with special needs?
- Minor children — at what age should assets be distributed from trust?
- Are there children you wish to exclude? (Requires careful documentation and sometimes a note from the client or physician)

**Asset Outline**
- Real estate (personal residence, investment property, vacation homes, vacant land, mineral interests): type, address, title holder(s), approximate value, mortgage balance

- Any specific bequests (items to specific people)?
- How should residuary estate be distributed?

**Fiduciaries**

- Successor Trustee (first choice + alternates)

#### Qualification Criteria
- Client has assets worth protecting or family circumstances requiring planning
- Client has mental capacity to execute documents (note any health concerns for file)
- Client can afford flat fee for services

#### Disqualification Criteria
- Client lacks testamentary capacity (cognitive impairment requiring evaluation; flag physician involvement)
- Undue influence concerns (family member pressuring client — attorney should meet with client alone)
- Matter requires probate litigation specialist if disputes already exist
- Multi-state real estate requiring attorneys in multiple jurisdictions

#### Conflict of Interest Checks
- Check if firm represented adverse parties (e.g., disinherited heirs)
- Spousal representation: firms often represent couples jointly; check for divergent interests
- For contested estates: check all family members against client database

- Prior will and/or trust documents (bring copies)
- Copies of all real estate deeds
- Recent financial and retirement account statements
- Life insurance declarations page
- Marriage certificates and divorce decrees
- Birth certificates for children
- Social Security cards or ITIN letters
- Prior power of attorney documents

  - Will + POA + Healthcare Directive (bundle): $800–$2,500
  - Revocable living trust + pour-over will + POA + Healthcare Directive: $2,500–$5,000+
  - Complex trust with tax planning (QTIP, ILIT, etc.): $5,000–$25,000+
- **Probate/estate administration:** Hourly ($200–$450/hr) or percentage of estate (2–4%)
- **Guardianship proceedings:** $2,500–$10,000+

#### Intake Duration
- **Phone screening:** 10–20 minutes
- **Initial consultation:** 60–90 minutes (clients often need time to discuss family dynamics)

#### Urgency Considerations
- **Terminal illness / imminent death:** Expedited to same-day or next-day; mobile signing may be required
- **Incapacity without POA:** Emergency guardianship proceedings possible
- **Probate deadlines:** Creditor claim periods (typically 3–6 months after date of death notice); tax returns for estates (9 months from date of death for federal estate tax return)
- **Estate tax:** Federal estate tax applies to estates over $13.99 million (2025); state estate/inheritance taxes have lower thresholds

#### Referral Patterns

- Multi-state property → attorneys in each state or coordination required
- Special needs planning → special needs trust specialist
- Medicaid planning → elder law attorney
- Contested wills/trusts → probate litigation specialist
- International assets → international estate planning specialist

### 1.8 Bankruptcy
*Covers: Chapter 7, Chapter 13, business bankruptcy, debt restructuring*

#### Standard Intake Questionnaire

**Personal Identification**
- Full legal name, social security number, date of birth
- Current address, prior addresses (last 2 years)
- Marital status; spouse's information (joint filing?)

**Employment & Income**
- Current employer, address, supervisor, position, length of employment
- Gross monthly income from all sources
- Spouse's employment and income (if joint filing)
- Last 7 months of pay stubs required (mandatory document)
- Prior 2 years of federal and state tax returns or transcripts (mandatory)
- Self-employed? Provide last 6-month profit/loss statement

**Assets**
- Real estate owned: address, approximate value, mortgage balance, name on title, if behind on payments
- Vehicles: year, make, model, value, amount owed, current on payments?
- Bank accounts: institution, type, balance
- Retirement accounts: type, balance (often exempt — critical to identify)
- Personal property: furniture, electronics, jewelry, collectibles (approximate value)
- Business interests, stocks, bonds
- Tax refunds owed to you?
- Inheritance expected within 6 months?
- Lawsuits pending where you may receive money?

**Debts**
- Credit card debts: creditor, balance, monthly minimum, current?
- Medical bills: total amount, individual creditors
- Personal loans: lender, balance, monthly payment
- Student loans (not dischargeable in Chapter 7 absent hardship)
- Tax debts (not dischargeable in most cases)
- Mortgage(s): current or behind? Number of payments behind?
- Car loans: current or behind?
- Child support / alimony owed (non-dischargeable)
- Any judgments against you?
- Are you being garnished, threatened, or repossessed currently?

**Prior Bankruptcy History**
- Have you ever filed for bankruptcy before? Chapter 7 or 13? Date?
- Was it discharged? (Affects timing for re-filing eligibility)
- Prior Chapter 7 discharge: must wait 8 years to file again
- Prior Chapter 13 discharge: must wait 4 years to file Chapter 7 again

**Business Activity**
- Have you operated a business in the last 6 years? What type? Still operating?

**Pre-Filing Requirements**
- Credit counseling certificate from approved agency completed within 180 days before filing (mandatory)

**Source:** [LegalAZ Bankruptcy Intake (PDF)](https://legalaz.com/client-intake.pdf), [Big Easy Bankruptcy Questionnaire (PDF)](https://www.bigeasybk.com/documents/BELG-BK-Questionnaire-v10_09.pdf), [JotForm Chapter 13 Intake Package](https://form.jotform.com/71531346884158)

---

#### Qualification Criteria

**Chapter 7**
- Must pass the Means Test: household income below state median, OR disposable income after allowed expenses insufficient to repay meaningful portion of debts
- Cannot have received Chapter 7 discharge in past 8 years
- No significant non-exempt assets (otherwise, consider Chapter 13 to protect assets)
- Mostly unsecured debt (credit cards, medical bills)

**Chapter 13**
- Regular income (employed, self-employed, pension, Social Security)
- Total debts below statutory limits (as of 2024: $2,750,000 combined secured and unsecured)
- Filed federal/state tax returns for last 4 years
- Not in active Chapter 7 proceeding
- Best for: saving a home from foreclosure; protecting non-exempt assets; stripping junior liens; catching up on secured debts

---

#### Disqualification Criteria
- Prior bankruptcy dismissed within 180 days for willful failure to comply or prosecution of fraud → automatic stay limited
- Income and assets make neither Chapter 7 nor 13 viable — explore alternatives (debt negotiation, workout)
- Tax debts primarily (Chapter 7 doesn't discharge most income taxes less than 3 years old)
- Student loans exclusively (not dischargeable without hardship proceeding)
- Pending criminal fines or restitution orders
- Fraud claims — non-dischargeable

---

#### Conflict of Interest Checks
- Creditors who are existing clients of the firm
- Check if firm represented any of the major creditors
- Trustee conflicts (particularly in smaller markets)

---

#### Documents Required at Intake
- Last 2 years of federal and state tax returns (mandatory)
- Last 7 months of pay stubs for all employed members of household
- Credit counseling completion certificate
- Bank statements: 3–6 months for all accounts
- Most recent mortgage statements
- Vehicle loan statements
- Any collection lawsuit documents
- Garnishment orders
- Deed(s) for real property
- Title(s) for vehicles
- List of all creditors with account numbers and balances (credit report helps)
- Recent statements for retirement accounts

---

#### Fee Structure
- **Chapter 7 (flat fee):** $1,000–$2,500 attorney fee + $338 court filing fee (2025)
- **Chapter 13 (flat fee or retainer):** $3,500–$6,000 total; portion paid upfront ($1,000–$3,000), balance paid through plan
- **Business bankruptcy (Chapter 11):** $10,000–$50,000+ in attorney fees; complex cases in major markets run $100,000+
- Many bankruptcy attorneys offer payment plans; Chapter 13 fees often built into the plan

**Source:** [Steiner Law Group — Chapter 7 vs 13](https://www.steinerlawgroup.com/news/filing-for-bankruptcy-chapter-7-vs-13/)

---

#### Intake Duration
- **Phone screening:** 15–20 minutes (assess income, primary debt type, urgency)
- **Full consultation:** 60–90 minutes
- Bankruptcy intake is one of the most document-intensive — many firms send intake packet in advance and require documents before consultation

---

#### Urgency Considerations
- **Foreclosure sale date:** Filing bankruptcy triggers automatic stay immediately, stopping the sale
- **Wage garnishment:** Filing stops garnishment immediately
- **Bank account levied:** Expedited filing can recover frozen funds
- **Eviction:** Bankruptcy may temporarily delay but not permanently stop eviction
- SOL on filing: Prior bankruptcy timing affects eligibility

---

#### Referral Patterns
- Business with complex restructuring → Chapter 11 specialist
- IRS tax debts → tax attorney (some tax debts dischargeable in bankruptcy after 3-year rule)
- Family law intersection (divorce + bankruptcy) → coordinate with family law attorney
- Consumer fraud → consumer protection attorney

### 1.9 Employment Law
*Covers: Wrongful termination, discrimination, wage disputes, harassment, FMLA*

#### Standard Intake Questionnaire

**Client Identification**
- Full name, address, phone, email
- Date of birth
- How did you hear about us?

**Employer Information**
- Company name, address, industry
- Number of employees (critical — Title VII applies to employers with 15+; ADEA applies to 20+)
- Your job title and duties
- Name and title of direct supervisor
- Your salary (current or at termination)
- Whether salary or hourly; overtime hours worked
- Fringe benefits received (health, dental, 401k, stock options)
- Your promotional history at the company
- Your disciplinary history (be honest — attorney will find out)

**Type of Claim (check all that apply)**
- Discrimination (check basis: race / sex/gender / age / disability / religion / national origin / pregnancy / sexual orientation / gender identity / other)
- Harassment / Sexual Harassment
- Retaliation
- Wrongful termination
- Workers' compensation retaliation
- Wage and hour (unpaid overtime, minimum wage, off-clock work, misclassification)
- FMLA/CFRA interference or retaliation
- Failure to accommodate disability (ADA)
- Whistleblower retaliation
- Non-compete / trade secret dispute

**Timeline of Events**
- Date of first discriminatory/harassing act
- Date of last act
- Date of termination / adverse action

- Names and titles of decision-makers who took adverse action
- Reason employer stated for action
- Why you believe the action was discriminatory / retaliatory

**Prior Complaints / Documentation**
- Did you complain internally about discrimination/harassment? To whom? HR? Written or verbal? Date?
- Did employer have an Employee Handbook? What did it say about the complaint process?
- Did you receive an offer letter, employment contract, or arbitration agreement?
- Performance reviews — copies available?
- Disciplinary notices — copies available?
- Did you sign any non-disclosure agreement?
- Termination letter or resignation notice?
- EEOC charge: Have you filed one? Date filed? EEOC number?
- Right-to-sue notice received from EEOC?
- Did you apply for unemployment? What was outcome?
- COBRA / health insurance continuation?

**Witnesses**
- Who will testify for you?
- Who will testify against you?
- Other employees treated similarly or differently?

**Damages**
- Lost wages: current salary; date of termination; current employment status and income
- Did you find new employment? When? At what salary?
- Emotional distress: diagnosed medical conditions related to workplace events?
- Out-of-pocket expenses related to job loss

**Criminal History**
- Any arrests, indictments, convictions (including DUI, minor offenses)

**Arbitration**
- Did you sign an arbitration agreement with this employer? (Critical — may affect forum)

**Source:** [Fett Law Employment Intake](https://www.fettlaw.com/intake-form/), [Carter Law Group Employment Intake](https://clgtrial.com/employment-intake-questionnaire/), [Philadelphia PCHR Employment Intake (PDF)](https://www.phila.gov/media/20210505100334/PCHR-employment-discrimination-intake-form.pdf), [Advocate Magazine — Employment Law Intakes](https://www.advocatemagazine.com/article/2023-march/employment-law-intakes)

---

#### Qualification Criteria
- Protected class involved (race, sex, age, disability, national origin, religion, pregnancy, etc.)
- Adverse employment action documented (termination, demotion, failure to promote, hostile work environment)
- Employer is large enough to be covered by applicable statute
- Within statute of limitations
- Right-to-sue notice received from EEOC (or close to 180/300 day deadline)
- Case has attorney's fees provision (FEHA, Title VII, ADA, FLSA — most do)
- Damages sufficient to justify contingency or hybrid fee

**NELA (National Employment Law Association) data: labor and employment firms accept fewer than 5% of cases presented — sometimes as low as 1%** due to case quality and volume. This makes qualifying criteria especially stringent.

**Source:** [Eve Legal](https://www.eve.legal/blogs/5-tips-to-improve-your-law-firms-intake-process)

---

#### Disqualification Criteria
- At-will termination with no protected class nexus
- Termination was clearly for performance reasons with documentation trail
- Client signed binding arbitration agreement (limits to arbitration, not court — different analysis)
- EEOC deadline passed without charge being filed (most employment cases require EEOC charge first)
- Right to sue expired: 90 days from receipt of right-to-sue notice to file federal claim
- No damages or insufficient damages to justify fee arrangement
- Client was independent contractor (many laws don't apply)
- Employer too small (under 15 employees for Title VII, under 20 for ADEA)

---

#### Conflict of Interest Checks
- Check employer against existing clients
- Check supervisor names against client database
- Check EEOC charge number against prior matters

---

#### Documents at Intake
- Offer letter, employment contract
- Employee handbook
- Any written performance reviews (positive and negative)
- Written disciplinary notices
- Termination letter (or resignation letter)
- Separation agreement and release (if offered — critical: DON'T SIGN without attorney review)
- EEOC charge and right-to-sue notice
- Pay stubs / W-2s (for damages calculation)
- Any emails, text messages, or other communications supporting claim
- Notes or diary entries contemporaneously made

---

#### Fee Structure
- **Contingency (most common for discrimination/wrongful termination):** 33–40% of recovery
- **Hourly:** $250–$500/hour for business-side employment law
- **Hybrid:** Reduced hourly + smaller contingency
- **Wage and hour class actions:** Plaintiff attorney receives fee from settlement fund (often 25–33%)
- **FLSA claims:** Prevailing plaintiff entitled to attorney fees from defendant

---

#### Intake Duration
- **Phone screening:** 15–30 minutes
- **Full consultation:** 60–90 minutes
- Critical: Review all documents before consultation when possible

---

#### Urgency / Statute of Limitations

| Claim Type | Filing Deadline |
|-----------|----------------|
| Title VII (federal) | 180 days to file EEOC charge (300 days in states with FEP agencies) |
| ADEA (age discrimination) | Same as Title VII |
| ADA (disability) | Same as Title VII |
| State claims (e.g., California FEHA) | 3 years to file with DFEH/CRD |
| FLSA (wage/hour) | 2 years (3 years if willful) |
| FMLA retaliation | 2 years (3 years if willful) |
| Right-to-sue expiration | 90 days from receipt to file suit |

**The EEOC charge deadline is frequently missed by clients who delay.** Attorneys must assess this within the first 5 minutes of any employment intake call.

---

#### Referral Patterns
- Non-compete/trade secret disputes → business litigation specialist
- Class action wage/hour → plaintiffs' class action firm
- ERISA / benefits claims → ERISA specialist
- Personal injury from workplace accident → workers' comp attorney

### 1.10 Tax Law
*Covers: IRS audits, tax liens, back taxes, tax planning, business tax issues*

#### Standard Intake Questionnaire

**Client Information**
- Full legal name, social security number, date of birth
- Address, phone, email
- Business name and EIN (if business matters)
- Type of entity (if business): LLC, S-corp, C-corp, partnership, sole proprietor

**Nature of Tax Issue (check all that apply)**
- IRS audit (correspondence / office / field)
- Unfiled tax returns
- Back taxes / assessed liability
- Tax lien (Notice of Federal Tax Lien filed?)
- Tax levy (bank account, wages, property)
- Wage garnishment
- Offer in Compromise
- Installment agreement (existing or needed)
- Tax court petition
- Penalty abatement
- Innocent spouse relief
- Criminal tax investigation
- Business payroll tax issues
- State tax issues (which state?)
- Tax planning / structuring

**Tax History**
- What years are at issue?
- Have you filed returns for those years? If not, why?
- Total amount owed (approximately)?
- Any IRS notices received? (Bring ALL correspondence)
- Tax transcript available? (Attorney can request via Form 2848)

**IRS Communications**
- Have you already spoken with IRS? If so, when and what was discussed?
- Have you made any admissions or given statements to IRS agents?
- Any upcoming IRS deadlines? (Audit date, CDP hearing deadline, Tax Court deadline)
- Notice type received: CP2000 / CP3219A / Notice of Deficiency / Notice of Intent to Levy / Notice of Tax Lien?
- 30-day letter received (Appeals)? 90-day letter (Tax Court petition window)?

**Financial Overview**
- Current employment and income
- Monthly essential living expenses
- Assets: bank accounts, real estate, vehicles, retirement accounts (for Collection Information Statement — Form 433-A/B)
- Do you have assets that could be sold to pay the debt?
- Current ability to pay: can you pay in full? Make monthly payments? Severe hardship?

**Documents Required at Intake**
- All IRS notices and correspondence (in chronological order)
- Last 2–3 years of filed tax returns
- Bank statements (3–6 months)
- Pay stubs, proof of income
- Business P&L and balance sheets (if business matter)
- Prior collection correspondence (levy, lien, garnishment notices)
- If audit: all records supporting the items under audit (receipts, logs, invoices)

**Source:** [Delia Law Tax Attorney](https://deliataxattorneys.com/irs-tax-attorney-in-the-bronx-ny-bronx-county-new-york/), [Rush Tax Resolution — Questions to Ask Tax Attorney](https://rushtaxresolution.com/nine-questions-to-ask-when-you-hire-a-tax-attorney/), [MyIRSTeam — How Tax Attorney Negotiates with IRS](https://www.myirsteam.com/blog/can-a-tax-attorney-really-negotiate-with-the-irs-heres-what-to-know/)

---

#### Qualification Criteria
- Significant tax debt (attorney fees justify representation — typically $10,000+ in dispute)
- IRS is taking enforcement action (levy, lien, audit) requiring immediate intervention
- Criminal tax exposure (requires attorney, not CPA)
- Complex tax dispute requiring legal argument (not just accounting reconciliation)
- Offer in Compromise: client's reasonable collection potential is less than assessed amount
- Client can pay attorney fees

---

#### Disqualification Criteria
- Small balance that can be resolved directly by client with IRS (under $10,000)
- Pure bookkeeping or accounting error → refer to CPA
- State income tax only in state where attorney not licensed
- Criminal charges requiring different specialist
- Client has no ability to pay attorney fees and doesn't qualify for LITC representation

---

#### Conflict of Interest Checks
- Check for representation of IRS agent or Revenue Officer involved in case (rare but possible)
- Check if firm has represented opposing parties in related matters
- Check for any business partner conflicts

---

#### Fee Structure
- **Flat fee by matter type:**
  - Audit representation: $2,000–$10,000 depending on audit type
  - Offer in Compromise: $3,000–$7,500
  - Tax court petition: $5,000–$25,000+
  - Installment agreement: $1,500–$3,500
- **Hourly:** $200–$500/hour for tax attorneys
- **Retainer:** $2,500–$10,000 for ongoing representation

---

#### Intake Duration
- **Phone screening:** 15–30 minutes (assess type and magnitude of issue)
- **Initial consultation:** 60–90 minutes
- Many tax attorneys offer free 30-minute initial consultation; fee applies if matters become complex

---

#### Urgency / Statute of Limitations

| Situation | Deadline |
|-----------|---------|
| Collection Due Process (CDP) hearing request | Within 30 days of levy or 30 days of NFTL lien notice |
| Tax Court petition (Notice of Deficiency) | 90 days from date of notice (150 days if outside U.S.) — missing = permanent loss of Tax Court rights |
| IRS audit SOL (IRS can audit) | 3 years from filing; 6 years if 25%+ income omitted; unlimited for fraud/non-filing |
| Refund claim | 3 years from filing or 2 years from payment, whichever is later |
| Offer in Compromise | Must file while collection SOL is open (generally 10 years from assessment) |

**The 90-day letter deadline (Tax Court petition) is one of the most dangerous in all of law.** Missing it permanently eliminates the right to contest without paying first.

---

#### Referral Patterns
- Criminal tax → criminal tax specialist (separate bar within IRS practice)
- State-only tax issues → state tax attorney or CPA
- International tax (FBAR, FATCA) → international tax attorney
- Business tax + entity restructuring → business attorney + CPA collaboration
- Payroll tax + employment law overlap → employment law attorney

### 2.5 Warm Transfers vs. Callbacks

**Warm transfers** (preferred):
- Intake specialist collects basic information, then transfers caller directly to available attorney
- Eliminates callback delay; highest conversion
- Requires attorney availability or "attorney on call" protocol

**Callback process** (most common but lossy):
- Intake takes message; attorney or intake specialist calls back
- Significant lead loss — prospects call multiple firms and hire whoever calls back first
- 78% of legal consumers hire the first firm to respond to their inquiry

**Key finding:** Voicemails and callback requests produce **74% drop-off rates** as prospects prefer instant engagement. ([Above the Bar Marketing](https://abovethebarmarketing.com/when-minutes-cost-six-figures-the-hidden-revenue-drain-in-law-firm-intake/))

### 2.7 Conflict of Interest Check Process

Every intake must include a conflict check before a consultation is scheduled. The process per [Clio's conflict check guide](https://www.clio.com/blog/conflict-check-how-to/):

1. **Collect prospective client information:** Full legal name (maiden names, nicknames, prior names), date of birth, address, opposing party names
2. **Search firm database:** Cross-reference against all current and former clients, opposing parties, related entities
3. **Analyze any hits:** Not all hits = conflicts; analyze whether representation would be adverse or involve confidential information
4. **Document the check:** Record date, time, person who ran check, results
5. **Determine outcome:**
   - No conflict: proceed
   - Potential conflict: escalate to conflicts counsel / senior attorney
   - Hard conflict: decline representation; issue non-engagement letter
6. **Timing:** Must happen BEFORE consultation in most jurisdictions

**Governing rules:** ABA Model Rules 1.7 (concurrent conflicts), 1.8 (specific prohibited transactions), 1.9 (former client conflicts). Non-lawyers can run initial checks; attorney must make the final determination.

### 4.2 Biggest Complaints About Intake Software

From law firm discussions and industry research:

- **Data silos:** Client info scattered across email, spreadsheet, CRM, case management — staff rekey data multiple times
- **One-size-fits-all forms:** Same generic intake form used for all practice areas; misses critical practice-specific questions
- **No conditional logic:** Forms don't adapt based on answers; clients wade through irrelevant questions
- **Integration failures:** Intake platform doesn't sync with case management system; data must be re-entered
- **Learning curve / low adoption:** 50% of lawyers don't fully utilize available legal technology; 44% cite resistance to change as primary barrier
- **Expensive with add-ons:** Clio Grow (intake module) is a separate cost from Clio; all-in can reach $3,000+/user/year
- **Clio Grow integration issues:** Popular option but adds cost to what users felt should be built-in
- **No real-time conflict checking** in intake tools — manual process creates bottlenecks
- **Limited mobile functionality** for on-the-go attorneys
- **No multi-language support** built in — requires workarounds
- **Poor client-facing experience** — intake forms designed for attorneys, not for clients who are often scared and confused

**Source:** [Reddit r/LawFirm discussion](https://www.reddit.com/r/LawFirm/comments/1rfxn4e/clio_vs_my_case_vs_practice_panther/), [Aderant — 7 Legal Tech Inefficiencies](https://www.aderant.com/blog/law-firm-inefficiencies/), [10 Law Firm Pain Points (2025)](https://nidish.com/law-firm-challenges-legal-ops-software-2025/)

### 4.4 Multi-Practice Intake Challenges

From [Law Ruler's Multi-Practice Guide](https://www.lawruler.com/blog/the-multi-practice-firms-guide-to-streamlining-intake-across-case-types/):

- **Different qualification criteria** for each practice area require staff to know multiple decision trees
- **Different urgency triggers** (SOL, arrest timing, court dates) vary wildly across areas
- **Different documents required** — PI needs medical authorizations; immigration needs visa documents; bankruptcy needs financial records
- **Different fee conversations** — contingency vs. hourly vs. flat fee requires different scripts
- **Staff training burden** — intake specialists who handle multiple practice areas must be trained across all of them
- **Information silos between practice areas** — staff handling PI intake don't communicate with family law team
- **One-size-fits-all forms cause loss of critical details** in specific practice areas

**Best practice firms use:**
- Conditional intake forms that branch based on practice area selected
- Standardized core steps (contact info, conflict check, consent) across all areas
- Practice-area-specific intake scripts and qualification criteria
- Separate intake workflows triggered by practice area in CRM
