### A3 — Welcome-Back Re-Engagement and Solicitation Dimension (Rule 4-7.18)

**Rule summary (verify):** FL Bar Rule 4-7.18 governs in-person, telephone, and real-time electronic contact solicitation. The "welcome back" re-engagement feature — where a returning prospective caller is recognized and re-engaged — may have a solicitation dimension if it is initiated by the firm rather than by the caller.

**Key distinction:** A caller who dials the firm initiates contact — passive receipt of an inbound call is not solicitation. However, a system that recognizes a prior visitor and actively re-engages them (e.g., "I see you called before — are you still interested?") may cross into solicitation, particularly if the prior contact did not result in engagement. The do-not-volunteer guardrail in DO-NOT-VOLUNTEER-PROMPT-BLOCK.md is partly designed to address this, but the solicitation analysis requires attorney judgment.

**The "SAMPLE" label is load-bearing but NOT sufficient on its own.** These appear to be illustrative deal documents — operating agreements, JV structures, potentially private placement memorandum-adjacent content — published to Diamond-tier members. Two distinct compliance dimensions apply:

**Dimension 1 — FL Bar Advertising:**
If these materials are used to demonstrate the firm's capabilities to prospective or current clients, they are advertising materials. Rule 4-7.13 (no misleading) and 4-7.12 (responsible attorney) apply. The "SAMPLE" stamp addresses outcome-guarantee concerns but does not substitute for responsible attorney identification and accurate representation.

**Dimension 2 — Securities Law (separate from FL Bar):**
The filename `SAMPLE_Small_JV_No_Reg_D.html` explicitly references Regulation D (the SEC's exemption framework for private securities offerings). Deal packages involving JV structures with investor/manager economics may constitute, or describe the preparation of, private placement memoranda or securities offering documents. This raises distinct questions:
- Is the firm publishing these as examples of legal work product in a context that could be read as offering securities or securities advisory services?
- Does the firm hold any FINRA/SEC registrations, or is this purely legal drafting work for clients?
- Are the sample documents gated behind Diamond-tier membership access? If so, is access controlled such that only existing clients (not the general public) can access them? Broad public access to deal documents containing financial structures may have securities disclosure implications depending on content.
